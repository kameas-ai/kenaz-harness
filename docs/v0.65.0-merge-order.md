## 0. How this document was built, and what "verified" means here

Every claim below is one of:

- **VERIFIED** — the cited `file:line` was opened and the content matched, or
  the quoted spec text was read at the cited line. Line numbers for
  production code were re-checked against the working tree, not copied from
  a spec.
- **SPEC-ASSERTED** — a spec states it; the statement is quoted and located,
  but the underlying code claim was not independently re-derived here.
- **UNVERIFIED** — listed in §7. Nothing in §1–§6 rests on an unverified
  claim.

The raw signal (a regex sweep of every `spec.md`/`plan.md`/`tasks.md` for
`core/…`, `frontend/…`, `scripts/…` paths) produced 1,100 mission↔path pairs
and 181 paths named by two or more missions. **Most of those are citations,
not edits.** CLAUDE.md's own warning — *"A naive Go grep over-reports ~3x"* —
applies exactly. The worked example: `entry-points-and-crash-reporting-01PMZD13`
appears in the raw sweep against `core/rpc/api.go`, `core/policy/cedar` and
`core/hooks`, but its `plan.md:154-157` says:

> **No unit touches `cmd/harness-vm`, `core/rpc/api.go`, `core/policy/cedar`,
> `core/hooks`, or any agentgraph manifest.** None of CLAUDE.md's usual
> shared-file conflict zones is in play.

So §1 is built from what specs say they will **touch**, not from what they
cite. Where a spec is vague, the file is in §7 (**UNVERIFIED**), not in §1.

## 6. The P0 lane

> **Ordering principle** (SJ01 tasks.md:40-47, and the owner directive):
> the owner ships **severity-ordered and continuously**, by **atomic shipping
> unit**, never by individual finding. *"A **unit** is the smallest set of WPs
> that leaves the tree **honest together**. Ship a unit whole or do not ship
> it — landing a proper subset leaves a surface asserting something the tree
> does not do."*
>
> And the severity scale is about **splitting**, not about the defect:
> *"**Severity below is the severity of shipping the unit's WPs *separately*,
> not of the underlying defect.**"* (SJ01 tasks.md:49)

### 6.0 ⚠️ "P0" does not mean the same thing in any two missions

**A naive P0-first sort produces a wrong lane.** Three incompatible encodings
are in use, all verified:

| Encoding | Missions | "P0" means |
|---|---|---|
| **Severity of the defect closed** | Z909 (`tasks.md:8-10`), ZA10 (`tasks.md:7-9`) | *"a live surface lies or a security property is absent"* / *"user data is at risk"* |
| **Severity of splitting or mis-ordering the unit** | SJ01 (`tasks.md:52-56`), Z202 (`tasks.md:30-31`), Z101 (`tasks.md:22-24`) | *"Splitting (or mis-ordering) the unit ships a lie, a hang, or an unenforced containment"* |
| **S0–S4 ladder, no P-scale at all** | HS01 (`tasks.md:25-33`), GA01 (`tasks.md:23-31`) | **S0 = security**: *"The tree ships a breach, or a downstream mission is blocked on containment"* |

The distortion is not theoretical. Two examples, both verified:

- **SJ01 UNIT-8 is P0 purely for ordering** — `tasks.md:67`: *"One WP; **the severity is the severity of landing it out of order.**"*
- **Z707 UNIT-1 fixes a cross-session authorization leak and is marked P1** (`tasks.md:25`), because Z707 has **no P0 marking anywhere** (`grep -nE '\bP0\b|\bS0\b' served-mode-is-a-real-mode-01PMZ707/*.md` → zero matches; mission severity is P1 at `spec.md:3`). Meanwhile **five missions mark a zero-code record unit P0.**

**A literal severity sort would therefore ship five decision records before the
authorization leak.** The lane below is ordered by **absolute defect severity
within dependency constraints**, and says where it departs from a mission's own
label.

Three missions have **no unit-level P0 at all** — Z303 (mission P1), Z707
(mission P1), ZD13 (its two P0s are `spec.md` *findings* V-1/V-2 at `:1222-1223`
which `spec.md:501,517` explicitly route **out** of the mission and into its
report). Two missions' only P0 is `UNIT-PI`, a **merge gate**, not a feature
unit — Z808 (`tasks.md:42`) and ZE14 (`tasks.md:39`); ZE14 adds *"This entire
mission sits below Z101's P0"* (`tasks.md:24`).
